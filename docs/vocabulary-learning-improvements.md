# Vocabulary learning improvements

The October 17 review follows dictionary selection through saving, practice and return navigation. The existing system already preserves separate meanings, dated evidence in four skills, FSRS scheduling, source attribution, personal edits and frozen sessions. Improvements should make the next useful learning action clearer without weakening those guarantees.

## Implemented in this batch

Dictionary meanings with an exact-bound diagram offer a View diagram disclosure before the learner saves or practises the meaning. Previewing does not create a saved meaning or learning evidence. Diagrams load into the rendered disclosure only when it opens. Existing identity, definition and part-of-speech checks still prevent artwork for another meaning from appearing.

The condition, scale, range and ground batch added 128 reviewed text senses, 15 contextual exercises and eight diagrams. The subsequent completion of the first six terms adds another 95 reviews, 16 exercises and seven diagrams, including 13 restored child meanings. The affect/effect, raise/rise and practice batch adds 111 reviewed records, 23 exercises and nine diagram bindings, including 42 restored submeanings. The mean, principal, principle, patient, adapt and adopt batch adds 84 records, 24 exercises and seven diagrams, including 13 restored meanings. The object, conduct, record, content and contract batch adds 76 reviews, 28 exercises and eight diagrams, including five restored meanings. The present, address, project, produce and refuse batch adds 100 reviewed records, 31 exercises and ten diagrams, including 12 reviewed restored meanings. At the end of that batch, the ledger contained 822 reviewed records out of 51,474 preserved or restored records, including three spelling references and two grouping references. This was progress, not dictionary completion. Content quality remains the largest constraint on useful practice.

Source organisation headings now carry explicit Meaning group metadata. Address exposed two imported headings whose child meanings had been lost; all 13 children are restored with revision evidence, with one awaiting a verified use example. Group headings remain searchable under All meanings without practice buttons. All new practice modes exclude them, and the initial learning-notes list prefers concrete meanings. The general address default now selects postal delivery details. Personal edits are checked before legacy default promotion, so a dictionary update cannot displace the learner's edited primary meaning.

Spelling-error records now have an explicit editorial reference flag. The dictionary shows a Spelling reference label instead of a practice button; selected saved records show guidance to choose a valid meaning. Every new exercise mode excludes the exact public reference. Original records stay readable, personal definitions remain independent and frozen sessions keep their original prompts and scoring.

Forty-four reading explanations in the eighth and ninth batches now have explicit example-type metadata. Their exact public text is labelled Reading note in dictionary and word details, with Reading cues for their associated phrase and interpretation hints, and is not used to generate a sentence-gap question. Replacing the example with a learner's own text removes that public classification. Other supported practice modes and existing frozen sessions are independent. This implements the first part of priority 3 below; annotations for older batches, pending-example metadata and learning priority still need work.

Direct entry navigation also revealed a loading race: starter or saved meanings could render before scoped teaching arrived, leaving the displayed dictionary and saved meaning on their original content. Word details now recompute when the catalogue becomes ready or changes, and dictionary meanings reapply teaching from cached source records. Search text and part-of-speech filters survive this refresh. Regression coverage verifies late arrival, source-cache reuse and preservation of personal examples.

The eleventh batch adds 165 reviews (127 original records and 38 restored children), 44 contextual exercises and 11 diagram bindings for lie, lay, bear and fair. The ledger reaches 1,137 reviews among 51,547 records, with 180 explicitly typed reading explanations. Fair now starts with equitable treatment while preserving personal choices. Bear’s source place heading leads to three independent geographical meanings rather than an exercise for a directory title. The batch also tests the first session with an empty collection for all four defaults in dictation and meaning recall.

A completed dictionary response no longer leaves an empty Other dictionary meanings (0) disclosure when all its meanings are already shown in learning content. Loading, retry and genuinely unavailable-dictionary feedback remain visible, and changing the excluded meanings restores the section without another fetch.

The expanded terms reinforce the selection problem: completed editorial notes include 46 preserved lay records and 61 bear records after restoration, spanning everyday verbs, history, specialist terminology and names. Reviewed coverage alone is insufficient for a learning order. A sense-level learning-priority field should be authored explicitly, with common meanings shown first and all source identities still reachable. Example readiness, register and diagram availability should remain separate signals.

The twelfth batch adds 173 reviewed records (130 originals and 43 restored children), 48 distinction exercises, 63 typed reading explanations and 14 diagram bindings for light, right, left and table. The ledger now reaches 1,310 reviewed records out of 51,590. The same spelling can require different valid actions: table a motion has opposing agenda meanings across contexts, and left can describe direction, departure or what remains. An exercise recommendation must retain the exact selected sense and its register/context rather than choose solely by term.

The thirteenth batch adds 331 reviews (130 preserved records and 201 restored children), 86 distinction exercises, 191 typed reading explanations and 20 diagram bindings for check, point, draw and sound. The ledger reaches 1,641 reviewed records among 51,791. Draw previously exposed six source directories while omitting their concrete children; it now starts with picture-making and retains every directory as a non-practice reference. Point's percentage-point comparison and backgammon board division, check's poker condition and sound's logical soundness have explicit corrections. Diagrams distinguish particle oscillation from wave travel and vessel draught from water depth.

The chess-check diagram keeps its conventional black and white piece colors in both themes. Theme contrast no longer reverses the sides described by the labels and explanation.

Source-heading validation preserves the original heading level while removing comments and sense identifiers. A nested heading cannot qualify as a top-level meaning group; regression checks retain valid top-level groups and reject second- and third-level headings.

These terms strengthen the need for an authored learning order: draw now has 166 records, including many obsolete and specialised constructions. Restoring them makes the dictionary complete for that term, but displaying all reviewed records as equally suitable study choices still overloads selection. The next product improvement should explicitly rank everyday learning senses, offer specialist and historical meanings through filters, and choose an available next exercise for the exact selected sense. Treat content readiness, priority and mastery as separate fields; reading an explanation must not count as demonstrated usage.

Continuous practice exposed recursive return URLs: each result-to-details-to-practice cycle encoded the entire previous attempt again. Result links now retain the exact session, selected sense, filters and source destination while omitting intermediate practice/detail wrappers. Learning-card position and its own parent return remain intact. Browser history still owns the immediate Back action; copied links can reopen the saved session and its source. Regression coverage repeats the cycle forty times and exercises the actual result-link renderer.

Reconstructing every retained release also exceeded the resource test's existing 15-second limit. The generator now reuses unchanged pack serialization and hashes within a single build, comparing ordered properties and shared source records. It precomputes baseline projections and indexes teaching, term and membership rows while preserving source order. The measured generation fell from 19.7 to 2.9 seconds on the local machine; all 1,756 complete resource hashes, URLs and discovery indexes stayed identical. The timeout remains unchanged.

The tenth batch adds 150 reviews (115 original records and 35 restored children), 33 exercises and ten diagrams for lead, minute, fine and match. At the tenth-batch release, the ledger contained 972 reviews among 51,509 preserved or restored records, with 97 explicitly typed reading explanations. Four further source headings and lead's phrase list are excluded from the initial learning-notes list and every new practice mode; the phrase list has its own Usage reference label. Fine opens with a concrete quality meaning. This improves meaning selection, but does not yet implement the learning-priority metadata proposed below. Entry-level audio also remains independent of the pronunciation contrasts now explained in the text and diagrams.

The first practice of a restored default now saves successfully even when the learner has not previously saved that child meaning. Validation recognises exact registered restored snapshots, while keeping entry ownership and stale-card checks. This closes a gap that the dictionary's save-before-practice route had hidden.

## Next priorities

The next implementation should prioritise common learning meanings over reviewed specialist material, then give a supported next-practice action for the selected meaning. These changes need explicit sense-level metadata and evidence-aware scheduling. Pronunciation must also be bound to a reviewed sense before homographs can reliably support audio-based practice. More diagrams alone would not resolve these selection and audio limitations.

| Priority | Observed limitation | Proposed behavior | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | With learning notes distinguishes enriched content, but reviewed historical, regional and technical senses appear beside everyday meanings. A fully reviewed word such as ground still has 39 senses. | Add editorial learning-priority metadata and subject/register labels tied to each sense, including general learning, specialist, historical and proper-name uses. Prefer general learning in the initial list, with explicit access to every other meaning. Keep source labels and never infer priority from definition wording alone. | Representative multi-sense words show common study choices first; all original IDs remain reachable and searchable; personal choices and existing sessions retain their meaning. |
| 2 | Word details show spaced-success counts and expandable mastery requirements, but the learner must choose the corresponding practice mode separately. The home recommendation selects a due mode compatible with the saved preference. | Offer a next-practice action for the selected meaning and a skill with missing or due evidence. Explain the chosen skill and offer supported alternatives. Preserve due-date scheduling; do not award mastery for browsing or previews. | Links preserve entry and sense IDs; unavailable cloze/distinction tasks fall back explicitly; practice completion adds evidence only to the assessed skills. |
| 3 | Many additional source senses still lack usable examples, while the current filter reports only whether learning notes exist. The ledger now explicitly classifies 434 reading explanations and excludes their public text from sentence gaps; older batches still need classification. | Extend explicit example types to earlier reviewed notes and pending examples. Add content readiness and a queue ordered by editorial learning priority. | No fabricated example or unrelated diagram fills a gap; reading explanations are labelled; unavailable exercise counts agree with actual queues; pending counts remain independent of automatic checks. |
| 4 | Contextual distinction tasks are selected per meaning. Related meanings are not presented as a short comparison activity in the same flow. | Add small, authored comparison sets for recurring confusions, such as condition/health status/disorder, scale/range, and ground/grounded. Each question retains its own target sense and explanation. | Distractors are unambiguously wrong in context; retry and frozen-history behavior retain exact prompts; score never transfers evidence to an untested meaning. |
| 5 | Recovery tracks the failed mode and review rows can retain an error type, but the result screen largely presents that type as text. | Offer the next available exercise based on a confirmed error: hear and spell for dictation, compare meanings for sense confusion, contextual cloze for collocation. Keep existing spaced recovery rules. | The suggested task exists for the exact sense; incomplete material gives a valid alternative; unassessed self-rating never becomes verified usage. |
| 6 | Source-captured words retain source links, while dictionary examples can dominate meaning selection. | Bring the learner's original sentence and source location into sense selection. Keep source quotation, editorial example and personal note distinct. | The original context survives saving and reopening; return navigation reaches its source; changing dictionary meaning does not rewrite the captured sentence. |
| 7 | Pronunciation playback receives the entry and accent, without the selected sense. Homographs such as present, produce and refuse can have different stress or sounds. | Bind reviewed pronunciations to the exact sense and part of speech. Clearly identify entry-level audio when its meaning is unverified; do not infer a recording's sense from accent alone. | Noun/verb contrasts select the matching verified recording or explicitly state the gap; dictation and frozen sessions preserve the intended sound and meaning. |

These are implementation priorities, not completed features. Before changing recommendation behavior, use synthetic accounts covering due, wrong-word, edited-meaning, archived-word and incomplete-content states. Compare completed useful sessions and successful delayed retrieval, rather than counting page views as learning.
