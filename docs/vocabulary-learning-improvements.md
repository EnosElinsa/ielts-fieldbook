# Vocabulary learning improvements

Assessment: 17 October 2026, after the third dictionary review batch. These are priorities for subsequent work, not claims of completed features.

The system already has separate sense identities, spaced scheduling, meaning/listening/spelling/usage evidence, contextual exercises, mistake recovery, source annotations and saved personal notes. Its next improvements should use those foundations. Adding more modes or another generic mastery score would make the workflow harder to understand.

| Priority | Observed gap | Proposed behaviour | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | “With learning notes” measures editorial completeness, not usefulness. Reviewed historical senses and place names can appear beside everyday meanings. | Add explicitly reviewed learning priority and subject/register labels. Show common learner meanings first, with specialist, historical and proper-name uses available through clear expansion and search. Do not infer frequency from the existence of notes. | Common field-of-study, form-document and periodical-issue meanings are easy to find; every preserved sense remains accessible; counts identify the current scope. |
| 2 | Distinction exercises compare words, but a learner can still confuse two meanings of the same word. | Introduce contextual sense-choice exercises for a small set of reviewed pairs, such as a physical field versus a research field, and a grammatical subject versus a research subject. Bind every answer to a stable sense ID. | Only one answer fits each reviewed sentence; wrong choices explain the contrast; results and scheduling retain the tested sense; no score is transferred to another meaning. |
| 3 | Recovery already tracks the failed mode, and review rows can retain an error type. The result screen largely displays that type as text. | Offer a concrete next action based on confirmed error type: hear and spell for dictation, compare meanings for sense confusion, contextual cloze for collocation. Keep the current spaced recovery rules. | The suggested exercise is actually available for the selected sense; unavailable material yields another valid action; no unassessed self-rating is promoted to verified usage. |
| 4 | Words captured from a source retain source links, but dictionary examples can dominate the learning view. | Bring the learner’s original sentence and source location into the same sense-selection flow. Keep source quotation, editorial example and personal note visibly distinct. | The original context survives save/reopen and navigation back to its source; changing the dictionary choice never rewrites the original sentence. |
| 5 | Content coverage varies greatly across additional meanings. | Continue the strict review queue, prioritising common senses and confirmed source contexts. Add diagrams where they explain a particular relationship; keep a compact textual layout where artwork adds little. | Review accounting distinguishes preserved, restored, reviewed and pending records; each example and diagram fits its exact sense; previously published resources remain available. |

The current release addresses prerequisites: the importer keeps specific glosses, 31 lost submeanings are independently selectable, cached source records retain their IDs, and the new meanings have reviewed examples, collocations, distinctions and exercises. The ledger covers 228 of 51,385 raw-plus-restored senses. Dictionary completion remains an active goal.
