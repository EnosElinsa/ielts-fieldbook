# Vocabulary learning improvements

The October 17 review follows dictionary selection through saving, practice and return navigation. The existing system already preserves separate meanings, dated evidence in four skills, FSRS scheduling, source attribution, personal edits and frozen sessions. Improvements should make the next useful learning action clearer without weakening those guarantees.

## Implemented in this batch

Dictionary meanings with an exact-bound diagram offer a View diagram disclosure before the learner saves or practises the meaning. Previewing does not create a saved meaning or learning evidence. Diagrams load into the rendered disclosure only when it opens. Existing identity, definition and part-of-speech checks still prevent artwork for another meaning from appearing.

The condition, scale, range and ground batch added 128 reviewed text senses, 15 contextual exercises and eight diagrams. The subsequent completion of the first six terms adds another 95 reviews, 16 exercises and seven diagrams, including 13 restored child meanings. The affect/effect, raise/rise and practice batch adds 111 reviewed records, 23 exercises and nine diagram bindings, including 42 restored submeanings. The mean, principal, principle, patient, adapt and adopt batch adds 84 records, 24 exercises and seven diagrams, including 13 restored meanings. The object, conduct, record, content and contract batch adds 76 reviews, 28 exercises and eight diagrams, including five restored meanings. The present, address, project, produce and refuse batch adds 100 reviewed records, 31 exercises and ten diagrams, including 12 reviewed restored meanings. The complete ledger now has 822 reviewed records out of 51,474 preserved or restored records; this includes three spelling references and two grouping references and is progress, not dictionary completion. Content quality remains the largest constraint on useful practice.

Source organisation headings now carry explicit Meaning group metadata. Address exposed two imported headings whose child meanings had been lost; all 13 children are restored with revision evidence, with one awaiting a verified use example. Group headings remain searchable under All meanings without practice buttons. All new practice modes exclude them, and the initial learning-notes list prefers concrete meanings. The general address default now selects postal delivery details. Personal edits are checked before legacy default promotion, so a dictionary update cannot displace the learner's edited primary meaning.

Spelling-error records now have an explicit editorial reference flag. The dictionary shows a Spelling reference label instead of a practice button; selected saved records show guidance to choose a valid meaning. Every new exercise mode excludes the exact public reference. Original records stay readable, personal definitions remain independent and frozen sessions keep their original prompts and scoring.

Forty-four reading explanations in the eighth and ninth batches now have explicit example-type metadata. Their exact public text is labelled Reading note in dictionary and word details, with Reading cues for their associated phrase and interpretation hints, and is not used to generate a sentence-gap question. Replacing the example with a learner's own text removes that public classification. Other supported practice modes and existing frozen sessions are independent. This implements the first part of priority 3 below; annotations for older batches, pending-example metadata and learning priority still need work.

Direct entry navigation also revealed a loading race: starter or saved meanings could render before scoped teaching arrived, leaving the displayed dictionary and saved meaning on their original content. Word details now recompute when the catalogue becomes ready or changes, and dictionary meanings reapply teaching from cached source records. Search text and part-of-speech filters survive this refresh. Regression coverage verifies late arrival, source-cache reuse and preservation of personal examples.

## Next priorities

The tenth batch adds 150 reviews (115 original records and 35 restored children), 33 exercises and ten diagrams for lead, minute, fine and match. The ledger now contains 972 reviews among 51,509 preserved or restored records, with 97 explicitly typed reading explanations. Four further source headings and lead's phrase list are excluded from the initial learning-notes list and every new practice mode; the phrase list has its own Usage reference label. Fine opens with a concrete quality meaning. This improves meaning selection, but does not yet implement the learning-priority metadata proposed below. Entry-level audio also remains independent of the pronunciation contrasts now explained in the text and diagrams.

The next implementation should prioritise common learning meanings over reviewed specialist material, then give a supported next-practice action for the selected meaning. These changes need explicit sense-level metadata and evidence-aware scheduling. Pronunciation must also be bound to a reviewed sense before homographs can reliably support audio-based practice. More diagrams alone would not resolve these selection and audio limitations.

The first practice of a restored default now saves successfully even when the learner has not previously saved that child meaning. Validation recognises exact registered restored snapshots, while keeping entry ownership and stale-card checks. This closes a gap that the dictionary's save-before-practice route had hidden.

These are implementation priorities, not completed features. Before changing recommendation behavior, use synthetic accounts covering due, wrong-word, edited-meaning, archived-word and incomplete-content states. Compare completed useful sessions and successful delayed retrieval, rather than counting page views as learning.
